Here's a question I ask every business I work with: what does it *feel* like to be your customer? Not what do you sell, but what's the experience of walking in, spending time with you and walking out again? That feeling is a huge part of what people are really buying, and it's the thing most small business content leaves out entirely. Experience-based content is about showing people what it's like to be there, not just what's for sale.

## A product shot answers "what." An experience answers "why."

A photo of a finished sketch is a product. A photo of the artist drawing someone, in a moody purple room, with the subject sitting there as it happens, is an *experience*. That's the photo at the top of this post, and notice what it does: it makes you want to be the one in the chair. You can almost hear the room.

Product shots matter. People need to see what they're getting. But on their own, they answer only the "what." They don't tell anyone why they should choose you over the dozen other places offering something similar. The experience does. It's the atmosphere, the attention and the little moments that make your business feel like yours. When your content captures that, customers aren't just deciding whether they want the product. They're imagining themselves there.

## Find the moment of delight, then tell it as a story

Every business has a moment where the customer lights up. The first look in the mirror after a great haircut. The first bite. The reveal of a finished room, a finished piece, a finished design. Find that moment in your business, because it's the emotional center of what you do, and build [your shoot](/blog/what-to-expect-at-your-first-content-shoot) around capturing it.

Then think of it as a little story with a beginning, middle and end. Walking in. The thing happening. The reaction. Three shots, or a short video that moves through all three, and your future customer can picture themselves going through the whole experience. It's far more convincing than any description you could write.

Real customers are the most convincing part of <Term definition="Content that shows what it feels like to be your customer, not just what you sell: the atmosphere, the attention, the moment of delight.">experience content</Term>, so it's worth inviting them into the frame. Always ask first. Most people are happy to say yes when they're in the middle of a great time, and their genuine reactions say more than any staged moment could. The same goes for your staff: [put your team in the frame](/blog/put-your-team-in-your-content) too.

## Keep it grounded in what you actually do

There's one trap to watch for: experience content can get so moody and artistic that people can't tell what you actually sell. Beautiful light, interesting angles, lots of atmosphere, and a viewer left wondering whether you're a bar, a gallery or a perfume brand. The fix is balance. For every shot that's all about feeling, make sure there's one that clearly shows what you do. The experience pulls people in, and the clarity tells them how to say yes.

If you want to start somewhere, try this: write down the one moment your customers seem to love most about your business. Be specific. That's the centerpiece of your next shoot.
