When someone scrolls past your restaurant's post, you have about a second to make them think "I want to be there." Not just "that looks good," but "I want *that night*": the table, the drinks, the company, the feeling. That's what great restaurant social media content does. It [sells the whole experience](/blog/show-the-experience-not-just-the-product), from the first sip to the last bite, and it gives people a reason to make plans instead of just double-tapping.

## Light it like the room feels

The photo at the top of this post works because of the candle. Everything in it is warm, low and inviting, which is exactly how it feels to sit at that table at night. That's the single biggest lesson for restaurant and bar content: shoot your food and drinks in the light your guests actually experience.

So many restaurant photos are taken under harsh overhead lights or in the flat light of a mid-afternoon prep shift, and they end up looking like a cafeteria, even when the actual dining room is gorgeous. If your space comes alive at night with candles and warm lamps, that's the light your content should live in. When the light in your content matches the light in your room, every post becomes an honest preview of the evening someone could have.

## Show the moment, not just the plate

A cocktail sitting on a table is fine. A cocktail being poured, a toast mid-clink, a table of friends mid-laugh: that's a night out. Plates and drinks are the stars, but people are what turn them into an experience. Mix in shots with people in them, even if it's just hands reaching for a shared plate, and your feed starts to feel alive instead of like a menu.

Your menu itself is a constant source of content too. Specials, seasonal dishes, the cocktail only your head bartender knows how to make properly: each one is a reason to post and a reason for someone to come back. And don't forget the people who make it all happen. Regulars come back for the bartender who knows their order and the host who greets them by name as much as for the food. (More on that in [Put a Face to the Place](/blog/put-your-team-in-your-content).)

## Shoot ahead so you're never scrambling

Here's the hardest part of restaurant content: you're busy running a restaurant. Nobody has time to stage the perfect shot in the middle of a Friday dinner rush, and that's exactly when content tends to get skipped for weeks at a time.

The answer is to <Term definition="Capturing a month's worth of photos and video in one planned session, so you have a bank to post from instead of scrambling.">shoot ahead</Term>. One dedicated <Term definition="About four hours of shooting, planned ahead so every minute counts. Long enough for real variety, short enough that nobody's worn out.">half-day shoot</Term> a month can give you a bank of photos and video to post from for weeks, so you're not grabbing your phone between tickets or posting the same three photos on rotation. It also lets us plan around your best light, your best dishes and your quietest hours, rather than hoping the moment happens to line up.

If you want to see the difference for yourself, try this: pick your most beautiful dish or drink and shoot it at your best table, in the evening light your guests actually see. Then put it next to your last post. That gap is what good content can do for you.
