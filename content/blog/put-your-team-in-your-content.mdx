Think about the last local spot you fell for. Was it really the menu, or the product, or the price? Or was it the person behind the counter who remembered your order, asked about your week and made you feel like a regular on your second visit? For most small businesses, the people *are* the brand, and that's exactly why behind-the-scenes content for small businesses works so well. Your team is the thing nobody else can copy, and your content should show them.

## People connect with people

A photo of a drink is nice. A photo of the person who makes it, leaning on the bar and smiling like he's genuinely glad you came in, is an invitation. That's the photo at the top of this post. It puts a face to the place, and suddenly it feels like somewhere you'd want to spend an evening.

That's the power of putting your people front and center. Product photos tell customers what you sell. Photos of your team tell them what it's like to be there: who'll greet them, who'll take care of them, what kind of energy fills the room. For a lot of people, that's what tips the decision from "maybe someday" to "let's go this week."

## Your team is your differentiator

Anyone can sell coffee, cut hair, fix a sink or plan an event. There's probably someone down the road who does what you do. What they don't have is *your* people and the way your team treats customers. That's the real reason people come back, and it's worth showing over and over.

<Term definition="Content that shows what happens behind the counter or before the doors open: your team, your process, the real day-to-day.">Behind-the-scenes content</Term> is the natural way to do it. The morning setup. The inside joke between two staff members. The moment someone gets a tricky job exactly right. None of it needs to be polished. In fact, it usually works better when it isn't. It just needs to be real, and it needs to show the humans who make your business what it is.

## Making your team comfortable on camera

Here's the honest caveat: not everyone wants to be on camera, and that's completely okay. Some people light up the second a lens appears, and some would rather stay firmly in the background. Never force it. Start with whoever's excited, and let the others warm up in their own time. Hands at work, a laugh from across the room or someone mid-task without their face in focus all count as putting your team in your content.

When people are a little nervous, [everything that helps business owners on camera](/blog/how-to-look-natural-on-camera) helps your team too. Give them something to do rather than asking them to pose. Talk to them instead of directing them. Keep the mood light. On shoot day, that's my job, and it's the same approach that makes owners comfortable.

And always ask before you post. A quick yes from each person before their face goes online is simple respect, and it builds trust inside your team. People are far more enthusiastic about content they've agreed to be part of.

If you want an easy place to start, ask one team member this week if they'd be up for a quick "meet the team" post: one photo and one fun fact. (Want more ideas like that? Try [these fun content ideas](/blog/fun-content-ideas-for-small-business).) Post it and watch how your customers respond. Then ask the next person.
