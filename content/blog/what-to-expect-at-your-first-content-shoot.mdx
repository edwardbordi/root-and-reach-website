If you've never done a content shoot, the unknown is usually the scariest part. You picture bright lights in your face, someone barking directions at you, and you standing there trying to remember what people do with their hands. Most of the business owners I talk to have been putting this off for a while, and it's almost never because they don't see the value. It's because they don't know what they're signing up for. So let me take the mystery out of it and walk you through what to expect at a content shoot with me, from the first call to the content landing in your inbox.

## Before the camera: a conversation, then a plan

Everything starts with a conversation, and there's no camera anywhere near it. I want to understand your business: what you do, who your best customers are, what makes you different from the place down the street. And I want to know, honestly, what makes you nervous about being on camera. That last part isn't small talk. If you tell me you hate how your smile looks in photos, or that you freeze the second you have to talk to a lens, I can plan the whole shoot around that. Most of the discomfort people feel on shoot day comes from surprises, and the call is where we get rid of them.

The call is also where we figure out what the content is actually *for*. Are you trying to get noticed by people who've never heard of you? Show off a new space or a new service? Bring in more of a certain kind of customer? The answer shapes everything that follows, from the [location](/blog/photo-shoot-location-ideas) to the shot list to [what you wear](/blog/styling-a-brand-photo-shoot).

Then we plan it together. You're my creative partner, not my subject, and the message stays yours. I'll bring ideas (lots of them), but I'm not going to hand you a script that doesn't sound like you or a concept that doesn't fit your business. If the shoot calls for more than the two of us, I bring the people: a venue, models, another videographer, whoever the plan needs. You don't have to find any of it or coordinate any of it.

## The shoot itself: four hours, and more fun than you'd think

A shoot is a <Term definition="About four hours of shooting, planned ahead so every minute counts. Long enough for real variety, short enough that nobody's worn out.">half day</Term>, about four hours. That's long enough to capture a real variety of content and short enough that nobody's running on fumes by the end. You'll know the plan before we start, so there's no standing around wondering what's next.

Here's the part people never expect: it's genuinely fun. I talk to you the whole time. I tell you exactly what to do, and just as importantly, I tell you what you're doing well, because nobody relaxes while they're wondering whether they look ridiculous. We laugh a lot. Some of the best shots of any day happen in the in-between moments, when you've forgotten there's a camera in the room. One person I worked with put it better than I could:

> "i was nervous going into it, but she made it such a comfortable and wonderful experience."

That comfort isn't a nice extra. It's the whole strategy. When you're relaxed, [you look like yourself](/blog/how-to-look-natural-on-camera), and content where you look like yourself is the content that connects with customers.

## After the shoot: your content, and what comes next

After the shoot, you get your photos and video, <Term definition="The photos and video straight from the shoot, unedited. You get what we captured, ready to use as is or to edit however you like.">raw</Term> or edited, delivered when I say they'll be delivered. If you'd like <Term definition="Edited videos: trimmed, put in order and polished, so they're ready to post as they are. We agree on what you need, and the price, before I start.">finished cuts</Term> that are ready to post, we agree on exactly what you need, and the price, before I start editing. No surprises on the invoice, ever.

What you do with the content is up to you. Your social accounts stay yours. I'm not going to take them over, but I'm always happy to show you how to get more out of what we made.

And then we do it again. The real magic is the rhythm: one shoot a month. The first one is the one people worry about, and by the second or third, most clients are the ones suggesting ideas. Each shoot gets easier, the content gets better, and you never find yourself staring at an empty feed wondering what to post.

If you've been putting this off, the smallest possible next step is a quick hello. Tell me about your business and I'll reach out to set up a relaxed call. There's no pressure and no script, and by the end of it you'll know exactly what you'd be getting into. [Contact me](/contact) whenever you're ready.
