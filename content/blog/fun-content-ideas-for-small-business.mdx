Here's something I believe completely: if you're having fun, your customers can tell, and they want in. So much small business content looks like it was made under protest. Here's the product, here's the price, please buy it. And then owners wonder why nobody engages. The businesses people actually follow online are the ones that let a little personality through. So here are some fun content ideas for small business owners who'd rather show who they are than hide behind a logo.

## Why fun works

People buy from people they like. When your content shows a little play, it tells customers something a price list never can: what it actually *feels* like to walk in your door. Are you the warm place where everyone knows your name? The slightly chaotic, endlessly creative studio? The team that clearly enjoys each other's company? That feeling is a big part of why people choose you, and it's almost impossible to get across with product shots alone.

Fun also lowers the bar to <Term definition="On social media, engagement means people interacting with your posts: liking, commenting, sharing or saving them.">engaging</Term>. People will scroll past an ad without blinking, but they'll stop for a laugh, a behind-the-scenes moment or a team member being delightfully themselves. Those moments are often the ones that get shared, saved and remembered.

## Five fun content ideas to try

### 1. Show the in-between moments

The laugh right after the "real" photo. The team goofing around before you open. The photo at the top of this post isn't a product shot at all: it's three people with their feet up the wall. It's the moment *around* the work, and it's often the most human thing you'll ever post. Next time you're doing anything content-related, keep the camera rolling a little longer than you think you need to.

### 2. Let your team be themselves

[Your people are your best content](/blog/put-your-team-in-your-content). A quick "meet the team" where everyone answers one silly question (go-to coffee order, worst haircut, hidden talent) will beat a polished group photo almost every time. It gives customers faces and personalities to connect with before they ever walk in.

### 3. Turn your process into a show

Whatever you make or do, people love watching it happen: the pour, the cut, the before and after, the mess before it turns into something beautiful. Speed it up, add music, and let the work itself be the entertainment. It's a simple way to [show the experience, not just the product](/blog/show-the-experience-not-just-the-product). It doubles as proof that you know what you're doing.

### 4. Play with the trends, on your terms

Not every <Term definition="A trend is a format, sound or challenge that lots of accounts are posting at the same time. Joining in with your own spin can help new people find you.">trend</Term> fits your brand, and that's completely fine. Skip the ones that feel forced, and pick the ones that genuinely make *you* laugh. Then give them your business's own spin, so the trend becomes a vehicle for your personality rather than a copy of everyone else's.

### 5. Say yes to the weird idea

Some of my favorite shoots started with someone saying "this is probably silly, but…". Silly is usually memorable, and memorable is the whole point. If an idea makes everyone in the room grin, it's worth at least one take.

## Where fun can go wrong

Fun doesn't mean forced. If a joke doesn't feel like you, your audience will feel that too, and nothing falls flatter than a business trying to be something it isn't. The goal is your real personality, turned up a little, not a costume you put on for the camera. And keep reading the room: what's charming for a coffee shop might land differently for a funeral home.

If you want somewhere to start, try this this week: post one thing that isn't a product or a promotion. A laugh, a behind-the-scenes moment, your team being themselves. Then see what your people respond to. I suspect it'll tell you a lot about what your content should look like going forward.
