## The shift, stated plainly

Every small business is now expected to show up on social media with photos and video, and to keep
doing it. The owners who most need it are the ones least comfortable doing it: they are busy running
the business, they don't like being on camera, and "content creation" sounds like something for
influencers, not for a bakery or a contractor. The usual options don't fit them. An agency hands them
a strategy and a bill and keeps control of the message. A freelancer shows up, shoots, and leaves
them with a folder of files. Root & Reach exists for the owner in between: someone who wants to do
it, and do it well, but needs a partner who makes it feel easy and keeps it theirs.

## The primary reader

**The owner of a small, independent business who started it out of passion and is now being told
they have to "be on social."** One location or no location at all, a handful of employees or none.
They do everything themselves. They know their business is good and that it doesn't look as good
online as it really is. They have tried filming themselves on a phone and hated how it looked and
sounded. They have avoided booking a shoot because they don't know what it involves, what it costs,
or whether they'll look foolish. They aren't looking for someone to run their accounts. They want
great material they can use, made with someone they trust, without losing control of how their
business is presented.

Cristina's own words for who she works with: scrappy survivors, entrepreneurs with an idea, "little
people, not a big box", people invested in their business for more than the bottom line.

## Trigger events

- Opening, relaunching, or adding a new product or service and needing to announce it.
- A competitor's social presence suddenly looks far better than theirs.
- Being told by a friend, customer or advisor that they need to "post more."
- A season or event coming up (holidays, a grand opening, a busy season) with nothing ready to post.

## Objections — and how the brand answers

| Objection | The honest answer |
|---|---|
| "I'll look awkward on camera." | That's the whole job. Cristina makes the shoot comfortable and fun, and that shows in the footage. |
| "I'll lose control of my message." | You don't. She's a creative partner: she shapes it with you instead of handing you a script. |
| "I don't want to be locked into social media management." | She doesn't manage accounts. You get finished content, raw or edited, and you decide what to do with it. |
| "I don't know what it costs." | One half-day shoot a month, a clear price, and any editing quoted and approved before it starts. |
| "I need more than a photographer." | She brings the whole team when it's needed: models, venue, videographer, web, design. |

## Voice — do / don't

**Do:**
- Warm, personal, first person. The brand is Cristina.
- Encouraging and confident on the client's behalf: take the scary part out, never add pressure.
- Playful where it helps, plain where it matters (price, process, what happens next).

**Don't:**
- Agency jargon: "leverage," "synergy," "content strategy ecosystem."
- Influencer hype or pressure ("go viral," "10x your followers").
- Anything that makes the reader feel behind or judged for not posting.

## Secondary segments

- **Businesses with an existing team** (their own photographer or designer) who want Cristina to
  direct and bring the pieces together. Served by the creative network section.
- **Referral partners** (other creatives in her network) checking how she presents them.

## Explicitly NOT the target

- Businesses wanting full social media management, posting and community management.
- Large companies or chains with in-house marketing departments.
- People looking for free advice or free content. She works with paid clients only.
